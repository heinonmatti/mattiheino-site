The largest flood on record is, by definition, larger than every flood before it.
That single sentence should unsettle anyone who plans for the future by looking
at the past. We calibrate our defences to the worst thing we have already
survived &ndash; and then act surprised when something larger arrives.

Tail events are not rare versions of ordinary events. They live in a different
part of the distribution, where the usual intuitions about averages quietly stop
working. A system can look stable for decades precisely because it is
accumulating the conditions for its next large move.

Foresight, then, is less about prediction and more about building the capacity to
absorb what we cannot predict. The question is not *what will happen* but *what
must we be able to withstand*.
