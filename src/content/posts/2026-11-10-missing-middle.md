## Abstract

Attempts to reach multi-hazard preparedness can be hampered by an exclusive focus on individuals or households, similarly as an exclusive focus on large-scale structural factors can. Findings from complex systems sciences, as well as current best practices in intervention development, call for multiscale approaches which also consider the layers between these two ends of the spectrum. European survey data indicates citizens expect to primarily rely on family or friends in the first days following a disaster – an observation corroborated by the spontaneous emergence of prosocial mutual aid groups during emergencies. This presentation focuses on the “missing middle” between households and institutions; the role of community networks in preparedness. It reviews the current literature regarding influences on preparedness in networks, introduces the concept of evolutionary trajectories in preparedness capacity building, and showcases two complementary methods for navigating these trajectories: ego network mapping and participatory narrative inquiry. The presentation states a case for the importance of intervention portfolios generated and owned by communities. Preparedness activities in communities may not only enhance preparedness, but can produce the social cohesion needed for other resilience-building activities – such as community physical activity groups – which for some communities may be the kind of activities that open the door for preparedness enhancing initiatives.
